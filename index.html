Who Believes College Is Within Reach

Question
What shapes whether an eighth grader believes they have a real chance of going to college, and which gaps survive once effort and friend group are accounted for?
Data
The National Institute of Justice evaluation of the GREAT program (ICPSR 3337), a survey of 5,935 eighth graders across 42 schools in 11 US cities, analyzed on a complete-case sample of 5,677.
Method
Binary logistic regression in Stata. Built composite measures of school engagement and peer academic environment from six survey items, controlling for sex, race, and age. Tested the proportional odds assumption first and moved to a binary logit when it failed. Reported odds ratios, average marginal effects, and predicted probabilities, with model fit checked by likelihood ratio test and Hosmer–Lemeshow, and collinearity checked by VIF.
Finding
School engagement mattered most: a one-point increase nearly tripled the odds a student expected to reach college. Friend group mattered nearly as visibly, with predicted confidence rising from 63% among students whose friends were largely disengaged to 88% among those whose friends were all academically involved. Two gaps survived every control. Hispanic students had 44% lower odds than white students, and each additional year of age cut the odds by 19%, a pattern consistent with the lasting cost of grade retention.

Inc. 5000: What Drives High-Growth Companies

Question
What do industry, geography, revenue, employee count, and repeat appearances tell us about how the fastest-growing private companies in the US actually scale?
Data
The 2014 Inc. 5000 list: 5,000 private companies with four-year revenue growth, latest revenue, employee count, industry, city, state, metro area, and number of years on the list.
Method
Cleaned and inspected the data in R, auditing every column for missing values and dropping empty fields. Built summary statistics for growth and revenue by industry and by metro area, then flagged extreme performers using z-scores on both growth and revenue. Charted average revenue by industry, growth against revenue, growth by employee size band, and growth by repeat appearances in ggplot2, and carried the results into an eight-point interactive Tableau Story.
Finding
Pick the one result you would lead with. Which industry or metro stood out, or what the growth-versus-revenue relationship turned out to be.

Fake News Detection Predictive Model

Question
Can the text of an article alone distinguish authentic reporting from fabricated news?
Data
A labeled corpus of real and fabricated news articles.
Method
Text cleaning, null handling, feature engineering, and categorical encoding, then a bake-off across five classifiers: logistic regression, k-nearest neighbors, decision trees, random forests, and naive Bayes. Evaluated with accuracy, confusion matrices, and cross-validation.
Finding
No single classifier was good enough on its own. Stacking the five into an ensemble with XGBoost as the meta-learner reached 90.25% accuracy on the validation set, and the gains came as much from feature selection and encoding strategy as from the choice of algorithm.

The Great British Bake Off Analytics Dashboard

Question
What patterns in contestant performance show up across seasons, and which ones actually predict who stays?
Data
Contestant, episode, and challenge results across every season, cleaned and reshaped from a raw, inconsistent source.
Method
Joined five separate tables covering bakers, challenges, episodes, outcomes, and seasons, then ran summary statistics on ratings by season, contestant age by gender and season, and performance by theme. Built a custom palette drawn from the show’s own visual identity, produced treemaps and distribution plots in R, and carried the results into an interactive Tableau story.
Finding
Episode ratings in the dataset peaked in the middle years, averaging around 8.0 across seasons 3 through 7, then fell to their lowest in seasons 11 and 13 before season 14 recovered nearly all of it. Finals dominate the top-rated episodes. Among contestants, women were on average two years older than men.

Boston Airbnb Pricing by Neighborhood and Property Type

Question
How does Airbnb pricing vary across Boston neighborhoods, and does property type or location explain more of the difference?
Data
Just under 3,000 Boston Airbnb listings with price, neighborhood, property type, capacity, bedrooms, and review frequency.
Method
Cleaned the listings in R and dropped incomplete records, then built a hierarchical treemap of price nested by property type and neighborhood, plus an interactive D3 version. Narrowed the set to eight neighborhoods and filtered out sparse categories to produce the presentation layer in Tableau and Canva.
Finding
Which neighborhoods and property types commanded the highest prices, and anything that surprised you.

Coffee Quality, Origin, and Altitude

Question
Which coffee-producing countries earn the highest quality ratings, and does growing altitude help explain the difference?
Data
The coffee ratings dataset, scoring samples out of 100 total cup points alongside country of origin and mean growing altitude.
Method
Summary statistics of total cup points grouped by country of origin in R, ranked by mean score. Narrowed to the five highest-rated origins, compared altitude distributions across them, and plotted altitude against quality score by country in ggplot2.
Finding
Papua New Guinea earned the highest average cup score, with Ethiopia second and Haiti lowest. Add what the altitude comparison showed: did higher-grown coffee actually score better?
